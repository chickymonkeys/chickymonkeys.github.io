Religion is not just a private matter. Using full-count administrative data from IRS filings for nonprofit organizations covering tax years 2009 to 2023, we document its pervasive role in public life. Through text analysis of their missions and names, we find three patterns. First, while explicitly religious domains — such as churches and faith-based congregations — are almost entirely religious, we also uncover a substantial religious footprint in areas typically perceived as secular, including international aid and pregnancy-related services. Religious pregnancy organizations, for instance, emphasize crisis support with faith-based language, whereas secular counterparts focus on reproductive healthcare and access. Second, religious philanthropy is directed primarily toward human beneficiaries: animal welfare and environmental topics are almost entirely secular, while the topics in which religious organizations are most concentrated serve people — suggesting that religion shapes the direction of prosocial activity rather than simply its level. Third, mission statements reveal substantive value differences between religious and secular organizations, and organization names can also carry explicit religious markers in some cases, so surface identifiers capture only part of the underlying differences. Together, our findings provide a new, large-scale perspective on how religion shapes the goals, language, and scope of nonprofit activity in the United States.
